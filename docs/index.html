RAGAUDIT · OPEN SOURCE · RUNS ON YOUR COMPUTER

A quiz for your document-answering AI.

ragaudit writes questions from your own documents and checks two things separately: did your app find the right page, and was its answer right? Then it improves itself, tuning your settings on practice questions and scoring them once on a locked final exam.

1 · Install once
$ pip install ragaudit-cli
2 · Open the setup page in your browser
$ ragaudit ui

WHAT IT IS · FOUR VERDICTS

A right answer is not proof your search worked.

Every question gets two checks: did your app retrieve the page the answer came from, and is its answer right? Crossing them gives four verdicts, and each one points at a different fix.

FOUND THE PAGE
MISSED THE PAGE
RIGHT ANSWER
True pass
Worked end to end. This is the number to protect when you change anything.
Fix: nothing. Re-run after every change.
Lucky pass
Right answer from the wrong page: the model knew it from memory. Most tests count this as a win; ragaudit re-asks with no pages to confirm.
Fix: retrieval. Your search isn't doing the work yet.
WRONG ANSWER
Generation failure
The page was there and the model still got it wrong.
Fix: the prompt, how much text you pass, or the model.
Retrieval failure
Wrong page, wrong answer.
Fix: chunking, embeddings or top-k.
HOW IT WORKS · WHY THE SCORES ARE HONEST

Built so the score can't be gamed, even by the tuning loop.

The pictures at the top walk through it. These are the rules that keep the numbers fair.

1 · REWORDED QUESTIONS

No copying the page

Questions avoid the page's own wording, so plain keyword search can't match them for free. Names stay, since a question about the Redwatch Protocol has to say so.

2 · A SECOND MODEL

Every answer key is checked

A different model from the one that wrote the question confirms it is answerable from the page, unambiguous and correct. Questions that fail are dropped, not graded.

3 · LOCKED FINAL EXAM

Tuning never sees the exam

ragaudit improve tries settings on practice questions only. The exam is opened once at the end, for your old settings and the best new ones, and a big practice − exam gap is flagged as memorising.

4 · SAME QUIZ, FAIR TRENDS

Only re-test what changed

Unchanged pages keep their questions, so two runs compare on the same quiz. Edited pages get new questions, deleted ones retire theirs, and the chart marks the change.

5 · LUCKY-PASS ABLATION

Memory is caught, not credited

When an answer is right but the page wasn't retrieved, the question is asked again with no pages. If it is still right, it was memory, and it is reported as a lucky pass.

GET STARTED · 2 COMMANDS

Install once, then set everything up in your browser.

Open a terminal (Terminal on Mac, PowerShell on Windows), paste each line and press Enter. The setup page walks you through picking the AI, your documents and your app, then runs the check.

1Install ragaudit (once)
$ pip install ragaudit-cli
2Open the setup page in your browser, then click Run
$ ragaudit ui
PREFER THE TERMINAL? EVERY COMMAND
ragaudit doctorChecks Python, your AI settings and your adapter, and says how to fix anything missing
ragaudit init --framework demoWrites a ready-to-run adapter; use plain, langchain or llamaindex for your own app
ragaudit check --docs ./docs --adapter ragaudit_adapter.pyOne quick test (about 20 questions), then opens the report
ragaudit improve --docs ./docs --adapter ragaudit_adapter.pyThe self-improving loop: tunes settings on practice, scores on the locked exam
ragaudit check --docs ./docs --adapter ragaudit_adapter.py --note "what you changed"Re-test after a change; the note labels the run in the score history
ragaudit report --openRe-opens the latest report

Connect your own app

ragaudit init --framework plain (or langchain, llamaindex) writes ragaudit_adapter.py with one marked line: return your app's answer and the text of the passages it retrieved. Your documents are not re-indexed.

ragaudit_adapter.py
def ask_my_pipeline(question):
    result = my_rag.ask(question)  # your pipeline
    return result.answer, [s.text for s in result.sources]
ragaudit_adapter.py
retriever = vectorstore.as_retriever(search_kwargs={"k": 4})  # yours
llm = ChatOllama(model="qwen2.5:7b")  # or any chat model
ragaudit_adapter.py
query_engine = index.as_query_engine(similarity_top_k=4)  # yours

To let ragaudit improve tune it, list the settings it may try in KNOBS and build your app from them in make_adapter(config); the generated file shows where.

What you need

Python 3.11 or newer, a folder of .txt or .md documents, and either Ollama (free, runs on your computer) or an OpenAI-compatible API key. The setup page checks all three.

Where results go

Every run is saved in a .ragaudit folder next to your documents. That is what the score history reads.

Your data stays local

With Ollama, nothing leaves your computer. Reports are files on your disk, not web links.
